# Gita: product and technical research

Research date: 13 September 2026; revised for customizable challenge videos and finger-placement minigames. Scope: native iPhone app in landscape, standard four-string ukulele and standard six-string guitar first. This is a research-backed proposal, not a claim that unbuilt features already work. “High/medium/low” below are engineering feasibility judgments, not measured model accuracy.

## 1. Recommendation in one minute

Build Gita around this promise: **“Turn the part of a song you keep replaying into a short, guided practice loop, then show your progress in a video you are proud to share.”** A right-to-left string/fret highway, adjustable tempo, precise-but-fair rhythm feedback, automatic phrase replay, customizable 1–100-day challenge, and reusable video templates make a coherent product. **The user chooses the song or skill for every challenge entry; Day 1–100 is a personal content series, not a prescribed lesson schedule.** This is technically achievable when the app knows the intended notes. Uploading *any* song or tutorial video and automatically extracting a correct, playable guitar/ukulele tab is a separate, substantially harder product. Begin with authored/right-cleared lessons and structured tab import; treat audio/video transcription as a draft that the user checks.

This distinction matters because existing products already have tempo control and repeats: [Yousician’s Practice mode](https://support.yousician.com/hc/en-us/articles/201558362-Practice-and-Play-modes-in-guitar) restarts a song and offers tempo and wait-to-play controls, and [Rocksmith+ describes adjustable practice sections](https://news.ubisoft.com/en-au/article/5XfINjoRmVJgBKR8fGt4EM/rocksmith-accessibility-spotlight). Gita’s potential edge is *automatic micro-loops of the learner’s actual weak phrase*, placement-focused minigames, and a flexible song-by-song video diary—not merely another scrolling tab. That is a positioning hypothesis to test with learners, not a proven market advantage.

## 2. What the learner experiences

**First run:** welcome → optional Apple sign-in/skip → choose ukulele or guitar → auto-listening tuner → a three-to-five-minute *playable* tutorial → one successful mini-song. The interactive tutorial introduces the string lanes, fret numbers, what a chord means, count-in, beat, and a simple chord change in the context of playing. Avoid a long explanatory slide show. A mascot offers one concise instruction at a time, celebrates a recovery, and never hides what the player did wrong. Your existing Gita app has the first-run structure and a three-step microphone tutorial; it does **not** yet have the note highway, a repeat-practice engine, song import, or a 100-day journal ([current tutorial](/Users/kenziefubrianto/CH6/Mobile-App/Gita/Gita/Views/TutorialView.swift), [current lesson judge](/Users/kenziefubrianto/CH6/Mobile-App/Gita/Gita/Music/LessonJudge.swift)).

**After onboarding:** offer two clear doors, **Learn** and **Play a Song**. Learn is a browsable set of tutorials and minigames, not another onboarding or a required 100-day syllabus. Each lesson has “hear it → see the finger placement → try at a slow tempo → play with a beat → apply in a tiny musical phrase.” A finger-placement minigame can ask the learner to identify and form a chord shape before playing it. Play a Song opens a library of practice-ready arrangements and later the user’s imported arrangements. Either mode may be chosen for a challenge entry.

**During play:** four or six horizontal lanes move notes from right to a fixed hit line on the left. A note carries its fret number and duration; simultaneous notes form a vertical chord stack. Show the next chord shape early enough to move the hand, and distinguish “pluck individual strings” from “strum all indicated strings.” The player can choose **Practice** (pause or auto-loop at mistakes, slower speed, optional wait mode) or **Performance** (continuous song and a comparable score). Include a count-in when entering or restarting a short loop; do not make the player drag a video seek bar repeatedly.

**After play:** a brief result screen shows note/pitch correctness, timing, the hardest phrase, and the next suggested loop. The learner chooses whether this take belongs to a challenge day and which song it represents, then selects a reusable video template and previews the rendered clip before saving or sharing. “Day 17” is an editable series label associated with that chosen song, not a locked assignment. Multiple songs may be practiced on one day; each selected performance can have its own video. A private result remains available if the learner chooses not to record or share.

## 3. What is technically possible?

| Capability | Feasibility for an initial iPhone release | Why / condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Landscape note highway, count-in, tempo change, deterministic scoring, auto-loop | High | The song’s note events and beat positions are already known. Core work is a reliable audio/game clock and clear UI. |
| Single plucked note detection | High for clean, isolated playing; validate per device/instrument | The current app has a pitch detector; monophonic tools such as [CREPE](https://github.com/marl/crepe) and [SwiftF0](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.18440) are alternatives. They estimate pitch, not string identity. |
| Known chord / short strum judgment | Medium | Check *expected* pitch classes and onset against the known chord. Overtones, muted strings, room noise, and playback bleed require confidence-aware results. Do not claim every string was heard when only a subset was checked. |
| Unknown four- or six-string strum → all notes in real time | Medium–low | Multipitch transcription is possible in clean cases, but overlapping harmonics, attack noise, and missing fundamentals cause both false and missed notes. Benchmark on real users before product promises. |
| Unknown audio → exact string and fret for each note | Low from one microphone | The same sounding note can be played at more than one position. Timbre can provide clues, but a microphone-only system cannot guarantee the physically used string/fret. A tab or a fretboard camera/sensor would add information. |
| Import MusicXML with explicit tablature | High | [MusicXML’s `<string>`](https://www.w3.org/2021/06/musicxml40/musicxml-reference/elements/string/) and [fret](https://www.w3.org/2021/06/musicxml40/musicxml-reference/elements/fret/) fields encode what the highway needs. Validate tuning and file quality. |
| Import MIDI | Medium | Timing and pitches usually exist, but fingerings/string assignment need an arrangement algorithm and review. |
| Import a clean solo-instrument MP3 | Medium as an **offline draft**, not one-click publication | [Basic Pitch](https://github.com/spotify/basic-pitch) produces multipitch note events/MIDI and works best with one instrument at a time; it does not give trustworthy tab positions. |
| Import a full commercial mix or tutorial video | Low for automatic playable tab | Source separation, transcription, beat alignment, visual occlusion, and rights all compound error. A video of fingers does not necessarily show frets clearly. |
| Custom challenge entries and reusable per-song video templates | High, subject to capture/export validation | The day number, song, chosen take, and visual template are structured data; [AVFoundation can capture](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avcapturemoviefileoutput?changes=_11) and [compose media](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avassetwriter?language=objc). Rights and device performance remain separate checks. |
| Guided finger-placement minigame without camera verification | High | Diagram/touch interactions teach locations and shapes, but cannot establish that fingers are physically on the instrument. |
| Automatic physical finger-placement verification with one iPhone camera | Medium–low until tested | [Vision returns hand joints](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/detecthumanhandposerequest?changes=_5), not string/fret contact. Gita also needs fretboard detection, calibration, camera placement, and confidence handling. |

The precise conclusion is **not** “AI cannot do it.” It is “a single model cannot reliably solve every step, and Gita should not penalize learners for detector uncertainty.” A phone microphone can judge *whether expected pitches/timing are acoustically present*; it cannot prove the user used the displayed fingers.

## 4. How sound recognition should work

Sound is a changing waveform. A plucked string produces a fundamental frequency plus harmonics, and several plucked strings add their waveforms together. An FFT or constant-Q spectrogram is a picture of energy across frequencies over time; a bright peak is not automatically a separate played note, because it might be an overtone of another note. An onset detector asks *when an attack begins*; a pitch detector asks *which note(s) sound*; a tab mapper asks *which physical strings/frets could have made them*. Those are different problems. [Librosa’s onset tutorial](https://librosa.org/doc/main/auto_tutorials/01-intro/05-onsets.html) is a useful introduction to the first distinction.

**Recommended live pipeline for Gita:** microphone capture → noise/level gate → onset estimate → pitch candidates (single-pitch for isolated exercises, multipitch/chroma for chords) → compare against a narrow window of the **known expected score** → separate pitch and timing judgments → report a confidence level. If the detector is uncertain, show “try again” or omit an accuracy claim; do not display a false “wrong fret.” For a known G chord, the model only needs to answer whether the expected chord was audibly played near the expected beat, not transcribe the entire musical universe. For fingerstyle, compare expected simultaneous notes one-to-one so a single harmonic cannot satisfy two note targets. Use all required notes where signal quality permits, with configurable strictness; explicitly label any lenient chord match as such.

**Model choices to benchmark, not blindly combine:**

| Tool | Best role | Important limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| YIN/current pitch code, CREPE Tiny, SwiftF0 | Clean single-note exercises and tuning | One fundamental at a time; a strum may return only one pitch. [CREPE](https://github.com/marl/crepe); [SwiftF0 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.18440). |
| Basic Pitch | Offline multipitch candidate generator; possible on-device experiment | Spotify provides a [Core ML version](https://github.com/spotify/basic-pitch), but its repo says it works best on one instrument at a time. Core ML availability does not establish low-latency, accurate live strum judgment on Gita’s target phones. |
| MT3 | Offline, multi-instrument benchmark for imported songs | [Official MT3 repo](https://github.com/magenta/mt3) supplies pretrained checkpoints, but uses a research stack; note events still lack trustworthy guitar tab fingering. |
| TabCNN / guitar-tab models | Research baseline for position estimation | [Original TabCNN repo](https://github.com/andywiggins/tab-cnn) is older Python 2.7 research code and guitar-specific; not a production iOS drop-in. |
| Score-constrained chord templates/harmonic analysis | Cheap, explainable real-time baseline | Can be robust for known chords but not a general “all notes in any song” recognizer. Benchmark beside ML, not underneath it as unquestioned truth. |

For mixed music, a separator such as [Demucs](https://github.com/facebookresearch/demucs) may isolate a guitar-ish stem, but the official repository warns of bleed/artifacts and is no longer actively maintained; separation does not create accurate tab. Treat it as an optional offline experiment.

**iOS architecture:** [AVAudioEngine](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudioengine) handles microphone buffers and playback; [AVAudioSession `.playAndRecord`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudiosession/category-swift.struct/playandrecord?changes=_5) supports simultaneous input/output. Gita’s [current microphone](/Users/kenziefubrianto/CH6/Mobile-App/Gita/Gita/Audio/Microphone.swift) uses `.record`, appropriate for the present tuner/tutorial but needing redesign for song playback. Keep the audio callback lightweight, timestamp input/output, and run heavier model work off the real-time audio thread. Calibrate device/audio-route latency before judging “Perfect”; Bluetooth routing and speakers require testing, and headphones reduce backing-track leakage. Use [Core ML](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coreml/) only after measuring speed, battery, and accuracy on actual iPhones.

## 5. Content and import strategy

Use a structured **Arrangement** as the app’s source of truth, independent from the MP3. Minimum fields: instrument and tuning (including high-G versus low-G ukulele, capo, and guitar tuning); tempo/beat grid; sections/phrases; per-note beat, duration, string, fret, pitch, chord group, and technique; optional audio alignment; source and edit history. Store an explicit “position unknown” value rather than inventing a fret for imported pitch-only data. The renderer, lesson engine, score, automatic loop, difficulty, and recap all read the same arrangement. This avoids a separate transcription-only format that cannot drive gameplay.

Build imports in this order:

1. **Authored/right-cleared mini-songs and exercises.** Highest reliability and simplest to teach from. Use them to validate learning, audio scoring, and rhythm gameplay.
2. **MusicXML with tab, then a simple in-app correction editor.** MusicXML has explicit string/fret notation. Allow users to fix timing, lane, fret, and section boundaries before playing; mark edits as user-authored.
3. **MIDI plus position suggestions.** Let users choose between physically plausible fingerings; ask for tuning and capo. Do not pretend pitch implies one physical placement.
4. **User-owned solo-instrument audio.** Generate Basic Pitch/MT3 note candidates offline, estimate beats, offer an easy “tap to fix” review, then save a draft arrangement. A first success measure is “time to make a playable 20-second loop,” not transcription F1 alone.
5. **Video/PDF/tutorial transcription later.** [Apple Vision](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/detecthumanhandposerequest?changes=_5) can find hand joints, but turning arbitrary camera angles and occluded fingers into exact notes requires separate research. OCR of visible tabs may be easier than inferring fingers, yet still needs correction.

Do not build an arbitrary YouTube URL downloader: [YouTube’s API policies](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/developer-policies) prohibit downloading/importing/caching audiovisual content without permission and prohibit separating audio/video components through the API. [Apple’s review guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) also require rights to third-party content and authorization for downloading from services. A YouTube link can be a reference for the learner, but Gita should ingest only material the user has rights to import, or content licensed for the app. This is a product/legal risk assessment, not legal advice.

## 6. Rhythm scoring that is fun and fair

Use a stable audio-derived clock, not animation frames, as the time source. Define a *hit* as one expected note/chord group matched to at most one observed onset; its grade depends on time error after latency compensation. Keep **pitch correctness** and **rhythm** as separate dimensions, then calculate a combined performance score. A suggested prototype formula is 55% note correctness, 35% timing, and 10% completion/continuity; these are design defaults to A/B test, not a scientific standard. For chords, count notes individually where the audio evidence supports it, and give a distinct “chord quality” indicator rather than letting a single detected pitch award a full chord.

“Perfect / Great / Good / Miss” windows should be calibrated with users and hardware, not copied from Project Sekai or maimai. A 100 ms capture delay cannot fairly be graded as a late 100 ms performance. Show timing offset (“a little early / late”) only when reliable. Provide a training mode with forgiving judgment, optional metronome, speed from slow to full, manual loop handles, and automatic loop after repeated misses. Performance mode uses a fixed chart/speed/calibration version so personal-best comparisons mean something. Accessibility options should include left-handed lane orientation, large fret labels, high contrast, reduced motion, and a non-audio count-in.

Difficulty should be computed from a *specific arrangement*, not just song title: note density, tempo, syncopation, simultaneous-note count, fret span, chord-transition distance, and techniques such as barre, hammer-ons, or fingerstyle. “Easy” can be a one-string melody or a simple G chord; “hard” can mean fast timing or complex simultaneous positions. Provide easier arrangements of the *same* song where rights permit, so progress feels musical.

## 7. Finger-placement minigames: does Gita need computer vision?

**Yes, if the game must automatically verify where real fingers are placed. No, if it is teaching or quizzing the learner on a diagram.** Audio can confirm that a note or chord sounds plausible but cannot certify the precise finger/string/fret combination. Gita should never describe an audio-only result as “correct finger placement.” A useful progression is:

1. **Fret Finder:** the mascot names “second string, fret 1”; the player taps that location on an on-screen fretboard and then plays it. This teaches the map and checks the sound, but the physical placement is self-checked.
2. **Chord Builder:** the player drags virtual fingertips into a chord diagram, sees a clear hand-position animation, forms the shape on their instrument, then strums. This teaches the shape, while audio checks only the sound.
3. **Camera Placement Challenge (research prototype):** the player puts the iPhone on a stand with the rear camera aimed at the fretting hand and visible neck. Gita asks for one static shape, estimates fingertip-to-string/fret positions, waits for a stable hold, then asks the player to pluck/strum. If fingertips are hidden or the neck is out of frame, the result is **“not sure / reposition camera,” not “wrong.”** Start with one or two open chords and a small fret region, not arbitrary fast fingerstyle.

The camera pipeline would use [AVFoundation video frames](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avcapturevideodataoutput?changes=_2) → [Apple Vision hand-joint observations](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/detecthumanhandposerequest?changes=_5) → a separate fretboard/string detector or one-time geometry calibration → fingertip-to-string/fret mapping → confidence across several frames → optional audio confirmation. Vision does **not** provide “pressed string 2 at fret 1” directly. A 2D view often cannot tell a hovering finger from a press; the player’s own hand hides strings. A [published guitar-fingering prototype](https://library.imaging.org/admin/apis/public/api/ist/website/downloadArticle/ei/30/9/art00007) illustrates the research path but used depth-camera data and describes occlusion/lighting problems; it does not establish that a single ordinary iPhone camera will work across real players. Optional removable visual markers might help calibration, but should not be required until tested.

This is best as a **slow placement exercise**, separate from full-speed note-highway scoring. Test camera setup usability (phone stands, screen visibility while playing, left/right handedness), lighting, different necks, and false “correct” calls before shipping a camera-graded mode. Offer the no-camera games regardless, and process camera imagery on-device by default with explicit permission. If the vision test fails, retain useful placement teaching without claiming automatic verification.

## 8. The customizable 1–100-day challenge

**The learner, not Gita, chooses the practice content.** They can start a named series such as “100 Days of Guitar,” choose a song or minigame for each entry, repeat one song across many days, switch to a new song, or do several songs in one day. Day number and calendar date are separate: Day 18 can be recorded after a break, and missing a date does not erase progress. The user may reorder or replace unpublished entries, choose which take represents the day, and decide whether an entry stays private or becomes a video. Recommendations from Gita (“retry the chorus at 70% speed”) remain optional.

Suggested flow: **choose today’s song → practice weak phrases with automatic loops → record one or more performance takes → review accuracy/timing and choose a take → pick a video template → preview/export/share → add it to the challenge as Day N**. A learner can create a video for each song played, not merely one video per day. Each song retains its own history and personal best; comparing scores is meaningful only for the same arrangement, tempo and scoring settings. A first/best comparison on the same song is optional, not forced for every entry.

Data entities should reflect this flexibility: `Challenge` (name, target count, start date, privacy), `ChallengeEntry` (day label, date, notes, selected song performances), `SongPerformance` (song/arrangement version, difficulty, score, duration, take IDs), and `ShareVideo` (take, template version, selected scenes, export metadata). The lesson curriculum remains a separate optional path. The daily action can be short—tune, repeat a difficult phrase, then record once—but Gita should not force a 20-second exercise or a preset Day 1–100 syllabus.

This is a product hypothesis, not a guaranteed path to mastery. A [music-practice meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4073287/) finds structured practice is associated with achievement, but practice time alone is an imperfect measure and correlation does not prove this app will teach effectively. A [gamification experiment](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S074756321630855X) found that different game elements affect motivational needs differently; make the mascot, levels, and challenge support competence and choice rather than pressure to preserve a streak. Test whether people truly practice and improve, not just post videos.

## 9. Song videos, templates, and privacy

**The intended challenge output is a video for each chosen song performance**, not only a static scorecard. Start with one reusable portrait (9:16) template for social media and allow a landscape export later. The template can combine the learner’s camera footage, their own instrument audio, an optional animated Gita note highway, day number, song title, instrument, chosen difficulty, and honestly labelled score. For example: a short “Day 12 / 100 · song title” intro, the selected performance clip, then a two-second “accuracy / next goal” outro. Let the user pick a section or full take, edit the day/title text, hide personal stats, preview, then export. Later templates could show first-versus-latest on the *same* song, split-screen hands plus highway, or a mascot-led intro/outro. A gameplay-animation-plus-recorded-audio video can be a fallback when the camera is declined; a still card is a fallback only, not the main feature.

[AVFoundation can record camera/audio](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avcapturemoviefileoutput?changes=_11) and [compose/export media](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avassetwriter?language=objc). Gita must prototype synchronized microphone detection and camera recording on physical iPhones, because simultaneously judging performance and capturing a clean video introduces audio routing, latency, heat, storage, and battery issues. [ReplayKit](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/ReplayKit) can record app UI/audio, but Apple notes incompatibility with `AVPlayer` content; an AVFoundation composition may be the more controllable template route. Do not promise one-tap video until that spike succeeds. Export should run after gameplay, not compete with live note detection. Save raw takes privately, ask for camera/microphone permission in context, permit deletion, and share only after an explicit preview/confirm action.

Do not automatically embed copyrighted backing tracks or a YouTube tutorial’s audio in social clips. Capturing only the learner’s instrument avoids copying a commercial master recording, **but does not automatically clear the underlying song composition**: the [U.S. Copyright Office distinguishes composition and recording rights](https://www.copyright.gov/engage/docs/recording.pdf) and explains that music in video can require separate permission. Exact requirements depend on jurisdiction, platform and licenses; obtain rights advice before making broad cover-song sharing promises. Prefer original or licensed practice music for built-in templates. If Gita later hosts public user videos/charts inside the app, Apple’s [user-generated-content rules](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) add reporting, blocking, moderation, and contact requirements. Keep early sharing through the iOS share sheet to users’ chosen social apps rather than operating an in-app social network.

## 10. Data, evaluation, and when to train

Do **not** ask beginners to write down every hertz or train a model before the practice loop works. Notes are pitch labels derived from frequency; instrument technique and timbre vary. Collect a small, consented evaluation set first: labelled single notes, two-note fingerstyle, simple four-/six-string chords, clean and distorted electric guitar, multiple real ukuleles, different rooms/phones, speakers vs wired headphones, and optional direct-input electric guitar. Include exact note onsets, intended tab, and what is actually audible. Split by *player and instrument*, not random clips from the same session, so the test measures generalization. Evaluate note onset/pitch precision, recall and F1 using a documented matching tolerance such as [mir_eval transcription metrics](https://mir-eval.readthedocs.io/stable/api/transcription.html), plus false chord passes, missed notes, real-time delay, and battery use. Separately run learner studies: minutes lost manually replaying a phrase, 7-/30-day practice retention, and improvement on the same phrase at the same tempo.

For the camera game, collect a *separate*, consented test set of labelled fingertip/fret/string positions across hands, guitar/ukulele necks, angles, lighting and occlusions; measure correct-placement precision, incorrect-placement recall, and “unable to judge” rate independently of audio scores. Keep raw videos local unless the learner explicitly opts into research data collection. Only then decide whether custom training is worth it. [GuitarSet](https://zenodo.org/records/1422265) has acoustic guitar audio with aligned string/fret and chord annotations; its instruments and players will not represent every ukulele or electric-guitar setup. [SynthTab](https://github.com/yongyizang/SynthTab) has much larger synthetic guitar arrangements, but its authors disclose rendering errors and license the dataset CC BY-NC 4.0. [Fraunhofer’s IDMT-SMT-Guitar](https://www.idmt.fraunhofer.de/en/publications/datasets/guitar.html) includes electric-guitar licks but is provided for evaluation under CC BY-NC-ND 4.0. Check each dataset and model-weight license with counsel before commercial training or distribution. If the errors cluster around a tractable gap—e.g. clean electric chord attacks—collect targeted examples and fine-tune for that task. A “universal exact-fret foundation model” should not be an MVP dependency.

## 11. Milestone plan and decision gates

| Milestone | Deliverable | Decision gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Practice-loop proof | One authored 20-second arrangement on ukulele and guitar; note highway, count-in, speed control, manual/automatic phrase loop, saved attempts | In 8–12 observed beginner sessions, can people learn the phrase without manually scrubbing a video? Is the UI readable while holding an instrument? |
| B. First shareable song video | Record a chosen performance, render one editable 9:16 template with Day N/song/instrument/own audio, preview, export | Can users produce a usable video on target iPhones without broken audio sync, overheated devices, or an unclear rights path? |
| C. Finger-placement game and camera spike | Fret Finder and Chord Builder, then a fixed-camera test for one static chord using hand pose plus fretboard calibration | Can camera users position the iPhone naturally? Are false “correct” placement calls rare enough to be trustworthy? If not, keep camera mode experimental. |
| D. Trustworthy sound feedback and beginner journey | Single-note plus *known-chord* judgment, latency calibration, “uncertain” state, more playable lessons and gentle mascot | Compare with human-labelled recordings across devices. Do beginners reach a first musical success and feel scored fairly? |
| E. Custom challenge | User-chosen songs for Days 1–100, multiple song videos per day, take selection, private history, optional recommendations | Can users plan and publish their own series without a forced curriculum or streak pressure? |
| F. User song import | MusicXML/tab import and visual editor; MIDI suggestions later | Can a learner create a correct 20-second playable loop in a few minutes, with minimal expert help? |
| G. Advanced research | Solo-audio transcription drafts (Basic Pitch vs alternatives), richer camera placement, eventual video/PDF import | Ship only if measured errors and correction burden are acceptable; never silently publish guessed tabs or placement results. |

Recommended next build is **Milestone A followed immediately by B**, while running the small camera feasibility test in C before promising automatic finger verification. A addresses “I keep restarting YouTube because the sequence is too fast”; B addresses your clarified goal that *every chosen song can become a shareable challenge video*. Both create reusable data and media paths needed for later features.
